# Plot 2B: Human vs. Interstellar Technology

This plot provides a comparative look at the $\Delta V$ capabilities of existing human propulsion systems versus the requirements of a 3I/ATLAS rendezvous mission. It illustrates the vast gap between current "Deep Space" technology and what is required to perform maneuvers in the high-speed interstellar regime.

## What the Plot Shows
The bar chart compares several benchmarks:

### Human Benchmarks:
- **Apollo CSM (2.8 km/s):** The limit of manned lunar exploration.
- **Dawn Mission (11.5 km/s):** The gold standard for Ion propulsion (NSTAR), which took years of continuous thrusting.
- **Theoretical Ion Drive (~45 km/s):** The physics-based upper bound for high-ISP electric propulsion (assuming 10,000s Isp and a 10:1 mass ratio).

### Interstellar Benchmarks:
- **3I/ATLAS Intercept (5-27 km/s):** The "nudge" required to crash into Jupiter.
- **3I/ATLAS Rendezvous (~44 km/s):** The energy required to stop at Jupiter and enter orbit.

## Key Takeaway
The energy required to **stop** at Jupiter (Rendezvous) is roughly equal to the absolute theoretical limit of the most efficient propulsion systems known to human physics. This highlights the "alien technology" question: if an object is seen performing such a maneuver, it is utilizing energy levels and propulsion efficiencies that are at the very edge of, or beyond, current human engineering.
