# Plot 3A: The Rendezvous Porkchop Plot

This "Porkchop Plot" is a mission design tool used to find the optimal window for a spacecraft to rendezvous with Jupiter from the trajectory of 3I/ATLAS.

## What the Plot Shows
The plot maps the **Total $\Delta V$** required for a mission across two variables:
- **X-axis:** The date the spacecraft departs from 3I/ATLAS.
- **Y-axis:** The Time-of-Flight (TOF) in days to reach Jupiter.

The color contours represent the energy required. The "cooler" regions (darker) indicate lower $\Delta V$ windows.

### The Two-Burn Model:
1. **Maneuver 1 (Departure):** A burn to change the spacecraft's velocity from the 3I/ATLAS path to the transfer trajectory.
2. **Maneuver 2 (Arrival):** A capture burn at Jupiter (1.05 $R_{jup}$) to match Jupiter's orbital velocity, utilizing the **Oberth Effect** to maximize efficiency.

## Key Takeaway
Our high-precision scan reveals a global minimum $\Delta V$ of **~44 km/s**. This is an immense energy requirement—roughly double the escape velocity of the Sun from Earth's orbit. It reinforces that while "intercepting" 3I/ATLAS is possible, "stopping" to observe it at Jupiter is a monumental engineering challenge requiring advanced (non-chemical) propulsion.
