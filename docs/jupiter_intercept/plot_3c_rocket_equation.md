# Plot 3C: The Rocket Equation (Mass Ratios)

This plot translates the abstract $\Delta V$ requirements into a concrete engineering reality: **how much fuel do we need?**

## What the Plot Shows
Using the **Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation**, we plot the **Mass Ratio** ($m_{initial}/m_{final}$) against the total mission $\Delta V$ for various propulsion technologies:

- **Chemical (450s Isp):** LOX/LH2 engines like those on the SLS.
- **Nuclear Thermal (900s Isp):** High-thrust, high-efficiency speculative technology.
- **Electric/Ion (3000s Isp):** Like the Dawn spacecraft (Hall/Ion thrusters).
- **Fusion (10,000s Isp):** Advanced, theoretical propulsion.

## Key Takeaway
For the **Intercept Requirement (~5 km/s)**, standard chemical rockets are perfectly viable (Mass Ratio ~3). 
However, for the **Rendezvous Requirement (~44 km/s)**, the mass ratio for chemical rockets explodes to over **1,000,000**, meaning we would need a rocket the size of a small moon to stop a tiny probe. This proves that a rendezvous with 3I/ATLAS is only possible with **High-ISP technology** (Fusion or NTR).
